European Health Data Space (EHDS)

The EU's first sector-specific common data space. It gives every person access to and control over their health data across Europe, requires electronic health record systems to be interoperable and CE-marked, and creates a common regime for reusing health data for research, innovation and AI through permits.

The Regulation has been in force since 26-3-2025, but obligations arrive in phases: general application in March 2027, exchange of the first data and secondary use in March 2029, and the rest in 2031.

Chapter II

Primary use: patients' rights

Rights

  • Immediate, free access to their priority data, in a readable, downloadable format
  • Add information and request rectification
  • Portability to another professional, including in another Member State
  • Restrict professionals' access to part of the data
  • Know who has accessed them and when
  • Where the Member State allows it, opt out of access by professionals

Priority categories

Priority categories
Group 1Patient summary, ePrescription and eDispensation
Group 2Medical images and their reports, laboratory test results, discharge reports

They are exchanged in the European electronic health record exchange format (EEHRxF) via MyHealth@EU.

Chapter III

Electronic health record systems: a CE-marked product

An EHR system is any application or software that allows personal health data to be stored, intermediated, exported, imported, converted, edited or viewed. If it processes priority data, it is subject to a product regime.

1
Interoperability
  • European component that sends and receives in EEHRxF
  • No need to use the format internally
2
Access logging
  • European logging component
  • Who accessed what, and when
3
Conformity
  • Self-assessment with testing in a European environment
  • Technical documentation and EU declaration
CE
Market
  • CE marking and registration in the EU database
  • EU representative if the manufacturer is based outside the EU

Importers and distributors check the declaration, marking and information sheet. Medical devices and high-risk AI systems that claim interoperability with EHR systems must meet the same components. Wellness apps that claim it carry a specific label.

Chapter IV · from 26-3-2029

Secondary use: reusing data with a permit

Permitted purposes

  • Public health and health security
  • Policy-making and official statistics
  • Health education and training
  • Scientific research
  • Development of products and services, including training, testing and evaluating AI algorithms
  • Personalised healthcare

Prohibited

  • Decisions detrimental to individuals based on their data
  • Excluding from insurance or employment, or raising premiums
  • Advertising to professionals or patients
  • Developing harmful products
  • Re-identifying individuals
Secondary-use workflow
StepWhat happens
Data holderHospitals, public insurers, registries, researchers and industry companies describe their datasets with minimum metadata and make them available to the access body
ApplicationThe user applies to the access body for a permit, justifying the purpose, data and safeguards
DecisionWithin three months, extendable; it may grant, refuse or request more information
ProcessingIn a secure processing environment, with data anonymised by default and pseudonymised where justified; only anonymous results leave it
IndividualRight to opt out of the secondary use of their data, with public-interest exceptions

National application

Spain

Digital Health Act

The Ministry of Health put the draft Digital Health Act to prior public consultation on 22-9-2025. The proposed model:

  • The autonomous communities as regional digital health authorities, coordinated by a national authority held by the Ministry of Health, which represents Spain and connects to MyHealth@EU.
  • Regional access bodies for health data and a coordinating national body (the General Secretariat for Digital Health), connected to HealthData@EU.
  • A national interoperable digital health record and regulation of digital technologies in care.

As of November 2026 there is no record of the draft being approved by the Council of Ministers.

National Health Data Space

Presented on 29-1-2026 by the Ministries for Digital Transformation and of Health, with €70 million and the autonomous communities. It is a federated network of platforms, with a portal live since February 2026 and more than 330 datasets in its catalogue (August 2026).

The SNS Data Office acts as the coordinating access body and maintains the national catalogue. Cantabria is drafting its own Digital Health Act with a regional data space.

What it means for each player in the chain

Healthcare software

  • If it processes priority data: interoperability and logging components, self-assessment and CE marking (2029 or 2031 depending on the category).
  • Medical devices and AI that claim interoperability with EHRs: same components.

Prepare

  • Follow the EEHRxF implementing acts (based on HL7 FHIR).
  • Dataset metadata (HealthDCAT-AP).

As an importer or distributor of EHR systems

  • Check the EU declaration, CE marking and information sheet.
  • Do not distribute non-compliant systems.

Opportunity

  • Sovereign health node configurations for hospitals and regions.

Opportunity

  • Secure processing environments for access bodies and data holders.
  • EHR integration with MyHealth@EU and HealthData@EU.
  • Data catalogues and anonymisation.

Caution

  • If you develop or adapt an EHR system under your own brand, you may be the manufacturer.

Hospitals and health services

  • Data holders: describe their datasets and make them available.
  • Buy CE-marked EHR systems.
  • Guarantee patients' access rights.

Research and industry

  • Prepare permit applications with purpose, data and safeguards.
  • Work in secure environments: the data does not leave.
