NIS2 Directive

The European law that requires thousands of companies and public administrations in critical sectors to manage their cybersecurity risks, report incidents within 24 hours and control their suppliers. Cloud, data centres and managed service providers are covered as digital infrastructure.

Spain has not transposed NIS2: the deadline expired on 17-10-2024 and the Commission took Spain to the Court of Justice in July 2026. Meanwhile, Royal Decree-Law 12/2018 (NIS1) still applies, but customers are already asking for NIS2 in their contracts.

The essentials in three ideas

1By size and sector

It applies to medium-sized and large companies in 18 sectors. Some players are covered regardless of size: DNS providers, domain name registries, trust services and public communications networks.

Arts. 2–3 · Annexes I and II

2Management is accountable

Management bodies approve the measures, oversee their implementation, receive training and can be held liable for infringements.

Art. 20

3The supply chain counts

Supplier security is one of the ten mandatory measures. That is why NIS2 also reaches companies that are not covered: their customers pass the requirements on to them by contract.

Scope

Who is covered

The general rule is size-based: medium-sized companies (50 or more employees, or more than €10m in turnover and balance sheet) and large ones. Each Member State can add entities because of their critical role.

Annex I · sectors of high criticality

  • Energy, transport, banking, financial market infrastructures
  • Health, drinking water and waste water
  • Digital infrastructure: internet exchange points, DNS, domain name registries, cloud computing, data centres, content delivery networks, trust services, public communications networks and services
  • B2B ICT service management: managed service providers (MSPs) and managed security service providers (MSSPs)
  • Public administration and space

Annex II · other critical sectors

  • Postal services and waste management
  • Chemicals, food
  • Manufacturing of medical devices, computers and electronics, electrical equipment, machinery and vehicles
  • Digital providers: online marketplaces, search engines and social networks
  • Research

Article 21

The ten risk-management measures

Technical, operational and organisational measures proportionate to the risk, with an "all-hazards" approach. For cloud, data centres, MSPs and other digital providers, Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690 spells them out requirement by requirement.

  • Risk analysis and information system security policies
  • Incident handling
  • Business continuity: backups, disaster recovery, crisis management
  • Supply-chain security, including the relationship with each supplier
  • Security in the acquisition, development and maintenance of systems, including vulnerability handling
  • Assessing the effectiveness of the measures
  • Basic cyber hygiene and training
  • Cryptography and, where appropriate, encryption
  • Human resources security, access control and asset management
  • Multi-factor or continuous authentication and secure communications
In practice, the measure most felt outside covered entities is the fourth: security questionnaires, notification clauses and audit rights that NIS2 customers impose on their suppliers.
Article 23

Reporting of significant incidents

An incident is significant if it causes or can cause severe operational disruption or financial loss, or if it affects third parties with considerable damage. Regulation 2024/2690 sets specific thresholds for digital providers.

In Spain, until there is a new law, incidents are reported to the reference CSIRTs: INCIBE-CERT (private sector), CCN-CERT (public sector) and ESPDEF-CERT (defence). The NIS Cooperation Group adopted common templates in May 2026.

Supervision

Supervision, penalties and liability

Powers of the authority

  • On-site and remote inspections, security audits and analysis of evidence
  • Requests for information and orders to remedy within a deadline
  • For essential entities, temporary suspension of certifications or authorisations
  • Temporary ban on management functions for those responsible

Registration

Entities register with the national authority. DNS providers, domain name registries, cloud, data centres, content delivery networks, MSPs, MSSPs and digital platforms register in the Member State of their main establishment in the EU, and ENISA keeps a common registry.

Arts. 3.4 and 27

Transposition

Spain and neighbouring countries

Spain

The Council of Ministers approved the preliminary draft Law on Cybersecurity Coordination and Governance at first reading on 14-1-2025; almost two years later it has not been sent to Parliament as a bill. Royal Decree-Law 12/2018, which transposed NIS1, remains in force.

Meanwhile, the CCN published the specific compliance profile CCN-STIC 892, which maps the NIS2 measures onto the ENS, and INCIBE maintains a table of essential and important entities and guides for SMEs.

On 8-7-2026 the Commission referred Spain to the CJEU for failing to transpose, seeking financial penalties.

Other Member States

  • Germany: NIS2UmsuCG has been in force since 6-12-2025; about 29,500 entities had to register with the BSI by 6-3-2026.
  • Italy: transposed through Legislative Decree 138/2024; the National Cybersecurity Agency (ACN) has published baseline obligations and incident guidance and has started audits.
  • France: the Resilience bill (NIS2, critical entities and DORA) reached the plenary of the National Assembly in October 2026; ANSSI uses its ReCyf framework as a working document.
  • Ireland and the Netherlands: referred to the CJEU together with Spain and France.
Spain's failure to transpose does not exempt a Spanish company providing services in Germany or Italy: it is already bound there. And cloud, data-centre and MSP providers answer to the Member State of their main establishment.

Proposal COM(2026) 13 · procedure 2026/0012(COD)

The amendment proposed in January 2026

Presented on 20-1-2026 together with the proposal for a new Cybersecurity Act (CSA2), to simplify and harmonise. It is a proposal, not law in force.

What would change

  • A new category of small mid-caps (fewer than 750 employees and up to €150m in turnover), treated as important entities
  • Inclusion of European digital identity wallet providers
  • More harmonisation of measures and reporting across Member States
  • Coordination with the CSA2 high-risk supplier framework
  • Twelve months to transpose the changes

And the Digital Omnibus

The Digital Omnibus proposal of November 2025 envisages a single incident-reporting point ("report once, share many") for NIS2, the GDPR, DORA, eIDAS and critical entities. It has not been adopted either.

What it means for each player in the chain

You may be covered

  • If you are medium-sized or large and manufacture computers, electronics, electrical equipment or machinery (Annex II).
  • If your software is a cloud service, it may be digital infrastructure (Annex I).

It always reaches you

  • Supply-chain questionnaires from covered customers.
  • Requests for vulnerability handling and notices, which the CRA will also make mandatory.

Risk

  • ICT distributors are not in the annexes, but NIS2 customers treat them as critical suppliers.
  • If you provide managed services to the channel, you may be an MSP.

Opportunity

  • A security catalogue aligned with the ten measures.
  • Channel training to sell NIS2 compliance with product and service.

Own obligations

  • Medium-sized and large MSPs and MSSPs are Annex I entities, with Regulation 2024/2690 as detailed guidance.
  • Reporting of incidents that affect your customers.

Service line

  • Scope and gap analysis against Art. 21.
  • 24/72-hour incident handling and continuity plans.
  • Management of the customer's supplier risks.

If you are covered

  • Management approval and training.
  • The ten measures documented and assessed.
  • Reporting procedure and relationship with the CSIRT.

What to ask suppliers for

  • Notification, audit and continuity clauses.
  • Security evidence (ENS, ISO 27001) and vulnerability handling.
