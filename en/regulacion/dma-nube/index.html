The Digital Markets Act reaches the cloud

The Commission preliminarily considers that Amazon Web Services and Microsoft Azure should be gatekeepers under the Digital Markets Act (DMA). If confirmed, they will have six months to comply with obligations on interoperability, data portability and no self-preferencing, including for their AI tools.

As of 14-11-2026 no decision has been published. The press, citing Bloomberg, places the designation in November 2026; the Commission has not set a date. Some headlines called AWS and Azure "designated" in June: it was a preliminary finding.

The essentials in three ideas

1No thresholds, by position

AWS and Azure do not meet the DMA's quantitative thresholds for cloud. For the first time, the Commission is using the qualitative route: an entrenched and durable position and a gateway role between businesses and their customers.

Art. 3.8

2The problem is leaving

The investigations point to switching costs, interoperability obstacles, conditional access to data, tying and unbalanced contractual terms.

Art. 19 investigation

3AI at the centre

The Commission notes that each cloud's AI tools and partnerships weigh more and more in the choice of provider and keep demand inside its ecosystem.

Amazon and Microsoft are already gatekeepers for other services (Amazon for its online marketplace and advertising; Microsoft for Windows and LinkedIn). The designation would add cloud to their list.

25 June 2026

What the Commission preliminarily concludes

The Commission's arguments

  • AWS and Azure are the first and second cloud services in the EU.
  • More than half of EU companies use cloud; these are an important gateway between businesses and their customers.
  • Entrenched and durable position: sustained leadership for years.
  • Scale, a locked-in user base and the weight of their AI tools in purchasing decisions.

The companies' response

  • Amazon: the assessment ignores the breadth of the cloud offering in Europe and may deter investment; the Data Act already regulates cloud.
  • Microsoft: questions why Google Cloud is left out.
  • CISPE (European providers): welcomes the finding but criticises the omission of Google.
The preliminary findings do not prejudge the outcome. If confirmed, the Commission will adopt designation decisions for Amazon's and Microsoft's cloud services.
Arts. 5 and 6 of the DMA

What AWS and Azure would have to comply with

DMA obligations apply to each designated service. For cloud, these are the most relevant; the third investigation may add specific ones by delegated act.

Obligations relevant to cloud
ObligationWhat it would mean for cloud
No self-preferencing (Art. 6.5)Not favouring own services (databases, AI, analytics) over third-party ones in its own ecosystem
Interoperability (Art. 6.7)Effective third-party access to the same hardware and software features used by its own services
Data portability (Art. 6.9)Effective, continuous and real-time portability of user-generated data
Access to business users' data (Art. 6.10)Free access for businesses to the data they generate on the platform
No use of third-party data (Art. 6.2)Not using non-public data of business customers to compete with them
No tying (Art. 5.8)Not making access conditional on subscribing to other services from the same provider
Freedom to switch (Art. 6.6)Not technically restricting users from switching service or using third-party services
Application

Penalties and enforcement

Fines and remedies

  • Up to 10% of worldwide turnover; 20% for repeat infringement
  • Periodic penalty payments of up to 5% of average daily turnover
  • Systematic non-compliance: behavioural or structural remedies

Who enforces

The Commission alone, through DG Competition and DG CONNECT. National competition authorities can investigate and assist; in the cloud investigations the Commission is working with the Dutch authority (ACM). In Spain, the CNMC does not enforce the DMA but can cooperate with the Commission.

Analysis

The debate

For

European providers, think tanks and part of legal scholarship argue that competition law arrived late to a market where three hyperscalers hold more than 65%, and that the DMA is the tool to tackle customer lock-in and the tying of AI services.

Against

The companies concerned and some think tanks warn of overlap with the Data Act, risk to investment and a questionable legal precedent in designating without meeting the thresholds. Others criticise Google Cloud being left out.

What it means for each player in the chain

Opportunity

  • Equal access to AWS and Azure features and marketplaces compared with their own services.
  • Access to the data your software generates in those clouds.

What to review

  • Licences that restrict running your software on third-party clouds.
  • Dependence on a single hyperscaler's proprietary services.

What may change

  • Resale terms and the terms of the AWS and Azure app marketplaces.
  • Bundles that tie services together.

Opportunity

  • Multi-cloud and European cloud offerings with less friction.

Opportunity

  • Migration projects and portable architectures.
  • Integration of third-party services in designated clouds.

Caution

  • Effects arrive in mid-2027 at the earliest; do not promise results before then.

What you gain

  • Better portability and access to your data.
  • Freedom to combine services from different providers.

What to do

  • When renewing multi-year commitments, provide for clauses that let you benefit from the 2027 changes.
