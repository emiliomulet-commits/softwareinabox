DORA Regulation

EU financial entities must show that they can withstand, respond to and recover from technology incidents, and control their ICT providers through contracts. For the first time, European authorities directly supervise the sector's critical technology providers, including the large hyperscalers.

DORA is not just for banks. Its mandatory clauses (Art. 30) reach any ICT provider to a financial entity, even an SME: audit, data location, notification, exit.

Article 19 · Delegated Regulation 2025/301

Reporting of major incidents

The entity classifies each incident using the criteria in Delegated Regulation 2024/1772 (clients affected, duration, geographical spread, data, criticality, economic impact). If it is major, it reports to its national supervisor.

Articles 28–30

ICT supplier risk: what affects any supplier

Register of information (Art. 28.3)

Every entity keeps a register of all arrangements with ICT providers, in the format of Implementing Regulation 2024/2956, and submits it every year to its supervisor. The ESAs used these registers to identify the critical providers. In 2026 the CNMV published its operational reporting guide on 12 February.

Exit strategy (Art. 28.8)

For services supporting critical or important functions: documented and tested exit plans, identified alternatives and transition without service disruption. Using several clouds is an option, not an obligation.

Articles 31–44

Critical ICT providers

On 18-11-2025 the ESAs published the first list: 19 providers, including major clouds, core banking platforms and data providers. The list is reviewed every year.

How they are designated

  • Systemic impact if they fail and number of entities that depend on them
  • Systemic importance of those entities
  • Reliance for critical or important functions
  • Difficulty of substitution
  • Right to be heard before designation; possibility of voluntarily requesting inclusion

What it means

  • A lead overseer (EBA, EIOPA or ESMA) with joint examination teams
  • Inspections, requests and recommendations
  • Daily periodic penalty payments of up to 1% of average daily worldwide turnover, for up to six months
  • An EU subsidiary within twelve months if the provider is based outside the EU
  • Oversight fees

The Bank of Spain provides two full-time supervisors to the examination teams. Recommendations to a critical provider are communicated to the entities that use it, which must take them into account in their risk management.

Level 2

Technical standards and simplification

The regulation is now complete

With the publication of the subcontracting and TLPT standards in 2025, the planned set of technical standards is in force. The 2026 work is supervisory: registers, inspections and oversight of critical providers.

Digital Omnibus

The November 2025 proposal includes a single incident-reporting point for DORA, NIS2, the GDPR and other regulations. The financial sector (AFME, pension funds) wants more simplification and the ECB has asked for DORA to be taken out of that change. It has not been adopted.

What it means for each player in the chain

If you sell software or cloud to finance

  • A DORA contract annex with the Art. 30 clauses.
  • Audit rights and participation in testing.
  • Exit plan and data return.

Advantage

  • Data located in the EU and transparent subcontracting make it easier to enter the register without objections.

What they ask of you

  • Support and maintenance contracts compatible with DORA.
  • Information on the manufacturer's subcontracting chain.

Opportunity

  • DORA contract templates for the channel.
  • Catalogue alternatives that reduce concentration.

Contractual obligations

  • If you support critical functions: quantitative SLAs, tested continuity, unrestricted audit.
  • Notification and assistance in incidents.

Service line

  • Building the register of information.
  • Tested exit strategies and portable architectures.
  • A common DORA and CADA evidence pack.

Financial entity

  • Annual register of information kept up to date.
  • Incident classification and reporting on time.
  • Annual testing and TLPT if significant.

Critical providers

  • Incorporate the lead overseer's recommendations into its risk management.
  • Measure and manage concentration on a few providers.
