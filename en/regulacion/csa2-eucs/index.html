CSA2 and cloud certification (EUCS)

The Commission proposes replacing the 2019 Cybersecurity Act with a regulation that strengthens ENISA, speeds up European certification and, for the first time, makes it possible to designate high-risk suppliers from third countries and require their components to be removed from critical infrastructure.

It is a proposal under negotiation: the Council and Parliament do not expect an agreement before 2027. The EUCS cloud certification scheme, requested from ENISA in 2019, has still not been adopted.

The essentials in three ideas

1Geopolitical risk, not just technical

The Commission could designate third countries that raise cybersecurity concerns and list suppliers controlled by them as high-risk, with bans in key ICT assets.

Arts. 100 and 104 of the proposal

2The 5G toolbox, made mandatory

Mobile network operators would have to remove components from high-risk suppliers within 36 months of the list being published.

Annex II of the proposal

3Faster certification

ENISA would prepare each scheme within twelve months of the Commission's request; schemes could give a presumption of conformity and cover an organisation's cybersecurity posture.

Title IV of the proposal

ICT supply-chain security

A new horizontal framework for non-technical risks, applicable to the NIS2 sectors, which makes binding what the 5G toolbox left to each Member State.

1
Risk assessment
  • Coordinated EU-level assessments of critical services, systems or products
  • Identification of key ICT assets
2
Designation
  • Third countries that raise cybersecurity concerns
  • Lists of high-risk suppliers by implementing acts
3
Measures
  • Ban on using their components in key assets
  • Transparency, audited safeguards, limits on remote processing
4
Removal
  • Mobile networks: maximum 36 months
  • Fixed networks, satellite and other sectors: deadlines set by implementing act

Consequences for a high-risk supplier

  • Exclusion from public procurement of key ICT assets
  • Exclusion from Union funding
  • No right to apply for or keep European certificates
  • Excluded from drafting European standards
  • Exception procedures, right of defence and a public register of decisions

The criticism

Lawyers and the Spanish Chamber of Commerce question the legal basis in matters of national security and call for reasoned, reviewable decisions rather than blanket exclusions. Several Member States raised this in the Council. The telecoms sector estimates replacement costs in the billions.

European certification framework

What changes in certification

In seven years, the Cybersecurity Act produced only one adopted scheme. The proposal acknowledges that implementation has "stalled".

EUCC, the scheme that does work

Based on the Common Criteria (ISO/IEC 15408) for ICT products, with substantial and high levels. Amended by Implementing Regulations 2024/3144 and 2025/2462. ENISA publishes the certificates. The CRA provides for it to give a presumption of conformity.

ENISA with more tasks

Early warnings, support for supply-chain risk assessments, testing environments, assessment of harmonised standards and a pilot for accrediting cybersecurity skills.

European Cybersecurity Certification Scheme for Cloud Services

The EUCS: seven years of deadlock

What it is

A certification scheme for cloud services with three levels (basic, substantial and high). ENISA's first draft dates from December 2020.

Why it is stuck

France and other Member States asked, for the high level, for sovereignty requirements: headquarters in the EU, data in the EU and primacy of EU law over third-country laws. The May 2023 draft included them; later versions removed them and the scheme was left without consensus.

Meanwhile

  • France: ANSSI's SecNumCloud, with immunity requirements against foreign laws, required by law for public administration for sensitive data.
  • Germany: the BSI's C5 catalogue.
  • Spain: the ENS and its conformity certification are the reference in the public sector.
  • EU: the CADA proposal tackles, through assurance levels, the sovereignty question the EUCS did not resolve.
Ordinary legislative procedure

Where the negotiation stands

Council

The progress report of 22-5-2026 records doubts about EU competence in national security, the scope of the high-risk supplier mechanism and Member States' involvement in designations. The text is likely to be watered down.

Parliament

At first reading. The EDPB and the EDPS have asked for clarification on ENISA's processing of personal data and the competence framework.

The law firms and analysts consulted agree: political agreement not before the end of 2026 and more likely in 2027, with the removal deadlines (36 months) applying only after designation.
Spain

How it reads from Spain

Positions

The Spanish Chamber of Commerce (May 2026) asks that the measures should not create blanket exclusions or automatic replacements, but transparent and reviewable risk analyses that respect national competence in security. Cuatrecasas points to "legal grey areas" in the communications networks regime.

What to do about certification

Until there is a European cloud scheme, the ENS (medium or high level) and CCN-STIC 892 for NIS2 are the evidence that public and regulated customers in Spain ask for. The CCN is the national cybersecurity certification authority.

What it means for each player in the chain

Risk

  • Being designated high-risk because of third-country control: loss of the public market and of certification.
  • Designated third-party components inside your product.

Opportunity

  • European certification with presumption of conformity (CRA and others).
  • Transparency of ownership and supply chain as a selling point.

Risk

  • Stock and support contracts for brands that may be designated.
  • Exclusion from tenders for key assets.

What to do

  • Map of supplier dependencies by controlling country.
  • Alternatives in the catalogue for key assets of NIS2 customers.

Risk

  • Projects for NIS2 customers with components that must be removed.
  • Responsibility in designing architectures with key assets.

Service line

  • Inventory of components by supplier and country.
  • Replacement and migration plans.
  • Support with certification (ENS, EUCC, future schemes).

If you are an NIS2 entity

  • You could be required to remove components from key assets.
  • Mobile telecommunications: maximum deadline of 36 months.

What to do now

  • Inventory of third-country suppliers in critical assets.
  • Replacement and exit clauses in new contracts.
