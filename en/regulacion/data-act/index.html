Data Regulation (Data Act)

It decides who may use the data generated by connected products, sets fair rules for data contracts between businesses, lets the public sector request data in exceptional situations and requires cloud providers to make switching provider easier, which will be free of charge from 12-1-2027.

For the end customer, the Data Act is a negotiating lever: leaving a cloud provider stops costing money from January 2027.

The essentials in three ideas

1Usage data belongs to the user

Whoever uses a connected product (a machine, a vehicle, a managed UPS) has the right to access the data it generates and to share it with a third party, such as a repair shop or an integrator.

Chapter II · Arts. 3–7

2Cloud without lock-in

Maximum notice period of two months, 30-day transition, migration assistance, functional equivalence for infrastructure and removal of switching and egress charges.

Chapter VI · Arts. 23–31

3Sovereignty over non-personal data

Data processing service providers must prevent third-country governments from accessing non-personal data where this conflicts with Union or Member State law.

Chapter VII · Art. 32

Chapter VI

Switching cloud provider: the customer's timeline

1
Notice · max. 2 months
  • The customer gives notice that it is switching or taking its data to its own infrastructure
  • Contracts cannot require longer notice
2
Transition · 30 days
  • Reasonable migration assistance
  • Extendable up to seven months if not technically feasible
  • Service continuity during the transition
3
Export
  • Exportable data and digital assets, with documented formats and methods
  • Retrieval period of at least 30 days
  • Secure deletion after exit
0 €
From 12-1-2027
  • Until that date, at most the direct costs
  • After that, no switching or egress charges
  • Exceptions for custom-built services and trial versions

Functional equivalence

In infrastructure as a service (IaaS), the source provider must do what is reasonable so that the customer achieves equivalent results at the destination. In platform and software as a service (PaaS and SaaS), the obligation focuses on open interfaces and interoperable formats.

Transparency

The provider must publish the jurisdiction of its ICT infrastructure and the measures it applies against access by third-country governments: information that is also useful for the CADA sovereignty assessment.

In the financial sector, the exit plan required by DORA and Data Act provider switching should be designed together: a single, tested plan.
Chapter II

Connected products: what changes for the manufacturer

Obligations

  • Design the product so that users can access their data simply, securely and free of charge (products placed on the market from 12-9-2026).
  • Inform, before the contract, which data the product generates, in what format and volume, and how to access it.
  • Respond to access requests from the user and from third parties the user designates.
  • Not use the user's non-personal data beyond what was agreed in the contract.
  • Comply with the GDPR when the user is a natural person or the data includes personal information.

Safeguards for the manufacturer

  • Trade secrets: it can require confidentiality measures and, in exceptional and justified cases, refuse access, notifying the competent authority.
  • The third party may not use the data to develop a competing product.
  • Reasonable compensation when the law requires it to make data available to other businesses.
  • Commission guidance on vehicle data (September 2025) as a sector reference.

Most active sectors: automotive and aftermarket, industrial and agricultural machinery, energy and smart meters, medical equipment and connected home appliances.

Under review

The Digital Omnibus and the Data Act

Proposal COM(2025) 837 of 19-11-2025. Until it is adopted, the Data Act applies as it stands.

What it proposes

  • Merging the Data Governance Act, the Open Data Directive and the Regulation on the free flow of non-personal data into the Data Act: a single text for the data acquis.
  • Strengthening the protection of trade secrets against access requests.
  • Easing provider-switching obligations for custom-built services and SME providers.

Where it stands

  • The Council approved its negotiating mandate on 21-10-2026 (document 14756/26).
  • The European Parliament is still preparing its position, with hundreds of amendments.
  • The negotiation is slower and more contested than that of the Omnibus on AI because it also touches the GDPR and ePrivacy.
Application in Spain and the EU

Authorities still being set up

Spain

At the date of this profile, there is no record of a formal designation of the competent authority or a specific penalty regime. Personal data issues remain with the Spanish Data Protection Agency.

Rest of the EU

In August 2026 only six of twenty-one Member States analysed had designated Data Act authorities. Germany passed its implementing law on 26-3-2026 and entrusted supervision to the Bundesnetzagentur.

What it means

The rights are already enforceable between businesses, including in the courts. The lack of an authority delays penalties, not obligations.

Reading by link

What it means for each player in the chain

Connected product

  • Inventory of the data generated by each product family (IoT, UPS, managed network equipment, servers with telemetry).
  • An access portal or API for the user and the third parties they designate.
  • Pre-contractual information sheets.
  • A documented trade secrets policy before the first request arrives.

If you sell cloud services

  • Contracts with a maximum of two months' notice and a transition plan.
  • A list of exit charges that disappear on 12-1-2027.
  • Documented export interfaces and formats.

In the catalogue

  • Information on the data each connected product generates and how it can be accessed.
  • Review of cloud resale contracts: notice periods, charges and exit assistance.

Opportunity

  • Fixed-price cloud-to-cloud migration services from 2027.
  • Connected-product bundles with user-ready data access.

New service line

  • Compliant migrations: inventory, export, equivalence testing and deletion.
  • Integration of customer machine data for maintenance, efficiency or AI.
  • Adapting contracts to the Commission's model terms.

As a third-party recipient

  • You can receive connected-product data when designated by the customer.
  • You may not use them to develop products that compete with the manufacturer's.
  • You must respect the agreed confidentiality measures.

Levers you already have

  • Ask the manufacturer for the data from your connected machines and equipment, or to give it to your integrator.
  • Negotiate your exit from your cloud provider: from 12-1-2027, free of charge.
  • Review data terms imposed by large suppliers.

If you are in the public sector

  • It can request data from businesses in situations of exceptional need, following a procedure and with a justification.
  • Combine the Data Act exit plan with the level-based migration envisaged by the CADA.
