## Annex B. Consolidated regulatory and channel timeline 2025–2030
### Why a single timeline
Between 2025 and 2030 the CADA coincides with deadlines under the AI Act, the Data Act, the CRA, NIS2, DORA, the DMA, the EHDS, Spanish legislation and changes to the large vendors' programmes. Vendors, distributor and integrators need a single view to plan investments, contracts and capabilities. CADA-related dates after 2027 are estimates based on this report's baseline scenario.

### Reading the timeline
The timeline shows two workload peaks for the channel. The first, in 2027, combines the Data Act's charge-free provider switching, the AI Act's high-risk obligations and the CRA's essential requirements, with direct obligations for importers and distributors: it is the year to adapt product onboarding processes, contracts and documentation. The second, in 2029–2030, concentrates the application of the CADA, the risk assessments and the migrations. The chain that uses 2027 to build a common compliance framework will reach the second peak with the capacity to meet demand.
> Two peaks, a single preparation: the evidence system the channel builds in 2027 for the CRA, the Data Act and the AI Act is the basis for the 2028–2029 CADA file.

## Annex C. Checklist for each player in the chain
### What it is for
Level 1 will be the entry requirement for most of the public cloud market, and levels 2 and 3 will depend on evidence provided by the whole chain. This list translates the requirements of Annexes II and III into practical questions for each player. It does not replace the legal text or the guidance that the Commission and the national authorities will publish [R1].
### Vendor
| Check | Indicative evidence | Criterion |
| Where are the product's telemetry, logs and diagnostics processed? | Technical documentation and configuration | C |
| Is there a local management mode or a management region in the EU? | Deployment guide | C |
| Can second- and third-line support be delivered from the EU? | Support contract and escalation matrix | H |
| Is there an SBOM and a CRA-compliant vulnerability policy? | SBOM and published policy | I |
| Is it guaranteed that customer data does not train AI models? | Contractual clause | F |
| Are the ownership structure and third-country access requests declared? | Corporate declaration | G |
### Value-added distributor
| Check | Indicative evidence | Criterion |
| Is there a sovereignty sheet per product family? | Sheet published on the partner portal | B, C, H, I |
| Are CRA conformity and marking verified at product onboarding? | Onboarding procedure | CRA |
| Are first-line support and spare-parts logistics delivered from the EU? | Support organisation and warehouses | B, H |
| Are there reference configurations per level? | Solutions catalogue | Annex II |
| Are vendors' material changes communicated to partners? | Alert procedure | Art. 23 |
### Integrator
| Check | Indicative evidence | Criterion |
| Is the entity providing the service established in the EU? | Deeds, company register, VIES | A |
| Are all infrastructure, backups and recovery in the EU? | Inventory and data centre addresses | B |
| Do the service's data, metadata and telemetry remain in the EU? | Flow diagrams and logs | C |
| Are all subcontractors with a direct contract declared? | Subcontractor register | H |
| Does cybersecurity reflect the state of the art? | ENS, ISO/IEC 27001 | E |

| Is the company an SME for the purposes of automatic recognition of level 1? | Annual accounts and headcount | Art. 17(3) |
> A negative answer does not prevent selling: it indicates the work still to be done. What matters is that each link knows its gap before the buyer does.

## Annex D. Glossary
### CADA terms
- **CADA (Cloud and AI Development Act):** proposal for a Regulation COM(2026) 502 on the development of cloud and AI in the Union.
- **Union assurance level:** each of the four sovereignty tiers (1 to 4) that a cloud service can reach according to the requirements of Annex II.
- **Third-country control:** the ability of an entity or state outside the Union to exert decisive influence over the provider; it is the factor that separates levels 2 and 3.
- **Declaration of conformity:** document with which a provider demonstrates level 1 of a service through self-assessment.
- **Recognition:** decision of the national authority confirming level 2, 3 or 4 of a service following an audit.
- **Central repository:** Commission register of recognised services.
- **Material change:** a change to the service or the provider that may affect its level and must be notified.
- **Public-sector risk assessment:** mandatory analysis identifying public-order activities and the level they require.
- **Public-order activity:** a public activity whose disruption or unlawful access would affect essential functions of the State.
- **Acceleration zone:** an area designated by a Member State to facilitate the deployment of data centres.
- **Strategic project:** a data centre that meets at least two of the five criteria in Article 14.
- **EuroCloud Federation:** voluntary federation of public bodies for sharing cloud capacity.
- **European added value:** non-price award criteria that reward EU technology and hardware.
### Channel terms
- **Broadline distributor:** volume distributor focused on logistics, stock and credit.
- **Value-added distributor (VAD):** specialist distributor with technical pre-sales, multi-vendor integration, training and services.
- **Two-tier distribution:** model in which the vendor sells to the distributor, which sells to the integrator or reseller.
- **Systems integrator:** company that designs, deploys and integrates technology solutions for the end customer.
- **MSP / MSSP:** managed service provider / managed security service provider.
- **Partner programme:** framework of tiers, competencies, incentives and benefits that a vendor offers its channel.
- **Deal registration:** mechanism that protects a partner's opportunity with the vendor.
- **CSP:** Microsoft programme for reselling cloud services through the channel.
- **Multiparty private offer (MPO, CPPO):** sale of software on a hyperscaler marketplace with a margin for the partner.
- **Consumption model:** pay-per-use infrastructure (for example, HPE GreenLake, Dell APEX, Lenovo TruScale).
- **ITAD:** IT asset disposition: retirement, certified erasure and reuse of technology assets.
- **Sovereignty sheet:** proposed by this report; a document per product family containing the sovereignty information provided by the vendor.
### Technical and market terms
- **Hyperscaler:** large global cloud infrastructure provider with massive computing capacity.
- **IaaS, PaaS, SaaS:** infrastructure, platform and software as a service.
- **Telemetry:** technical data that systems send about their operation, often to the vendor's support centres.
- **SBOM:** software bill of materials; an inventory of a product's components and dependencies.
- **HSM:** hardware security module for safeguarding cryptographic keys.
- **PUE:** power usage effectiveness, a data centre energy efficiency indicator; the closer to 1, the better.
- **Open stack:** a set of open-source software covering everything from infrastructure to applications.
- **Edge:** processing in nodes close to where data is generated.
- **Data space:** federated ecosystem in which participants share data under common rules without centralising it.
- **Connector:** component that enables a participant to exchange data in a data space while enforcing usage policies.
- **ODRL:** standard language for expressing machine-readable data usage policies.
- **OSPO:** an organisation's open source programme office.
### Other regulations cited
- **AI Act:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 on artificial intelligence.
- **Data Act:** Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 on data, which includes cloud provider switching.
- **NIS2:** Directive (EU) 2022/2555 on the cybersecurity of essential and important entities.
- **CSA2:** proposal for a new Cybersecurity Regulation (2026), successor to the Cybersecurity Act.
- **EUCS:** European cybersecurity certification scheme for cloud services, currently blocked.
- **CRA:** Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 on cyber resilience.
- **DORA:** Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 on digital operational resilience for the financial sector.
- **DMA:** Digital Markets Act.
- **EHDS:** Regulation (EU) 2025/327 on the European Health Data Space.
- **ENS:** Spain's National Security Framework (Esquema Nacional de Seguridad, Royal Decree 311/2022).
- **SecNumCloud:** French ANSSI framework for trusted cloud services.
- **WEEE:** waste electrical and electronic equipment (Royal Decree 110/2015).
- **VERIFACTU:** Spanish system of requirements for invoicing software, postponed to October 2028.
