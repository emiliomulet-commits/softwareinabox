## 13. DORA, the DMA and the reshaping of cloud programmes
### DORA and the oversight of technology providers
DORA is the first European regime to directly oversee technology providers. On 18 November 2025 the European supervisory authorities designated 19 critical ICT third-party providers, including AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft, Oracle and SAP, operators such as Orange and Deutsche Telekom and consultancies such as Accenture, Capgemini or NTT DATA [R46]. Financial entities must include clauses on location, access, audit and exit in their contracts.
### The DMA: AWS and Azure as gatekeepers
On 25 June 2026 the Commission communicated its preliminary conclusion that AWS and Azure should be designated as gatekeepers under the Digital Markets Act; if confirmed, they will have six months to comply with interoperability, portability and non-self-preferencing obligations [R47]. This may change licensing conditions and the ability to run software on third-party clouds, which also concerns the partners that sell that software.
### Hyperscaler programmes are already changing
Regardless of regulation, the large providers are reshaping their relationship with the channel:
- Microsoft tightened authorisation for the Cloud Solution Provider programme, with thresholds of USD 1 million in revenue for direct-bill partners and USD 30 million for indirect providers [R48], and has turned its marketplace into a route to market through the channel, with "resale enabled offers" generally available since November 2025 and multiparty private offers available in 30 European markets since May 2026 [R49][R50].
- In January 2026 Broadcom closed the VMware cloud service provider programme to many small European partners, and CISPE lodged a complaint with the Commission [R51].
- According to Omdia, partners will generate 59 % of hyperscaler marketplace sales by 2030 [R52].
### What it means for the value chain
The regulatory squeeze (DMA, DORA, Data Act and CADA) is pushing towards architectures with more deployment options. For software vendors, this requires licences that allow their products to run on European infrastructure and in customer environments. For integrators working with banking, insurance or fintech, it means answering DORA and CADA questionnaires with a single evidence package. For the value-added distributor, the reshaping of cloud programmes cuts both ways: Microsoft's thresholds and the closure of the VMware programme concentrate business in distributors with scale, and virtualisation and private cloud alternatives generate demand for new platforms, training and migration.
> The CADA does not act alone: the DMA, DORA, the Data Act and the changes to hyperscaler programmes all push in the same direction. The distributor with scale and an alternative catalogue gains relevance.

## 14. Data and data spaces: Data Union Strategy, Simpl, EHDS and Gaia-X
### The data strategy behind the CADA
The Data Union Strategy, presented on 19 November 2025, steers data policy towards implementation: more access to quality data for AI, simpler rules and protection of sovereignty. It includes "Data Labs" connecting the common data spaces with the AI Factories and a EUR 100 million boost for data spaces from 2026, and lists the CADA among its flagship actions [R53].
### Data space infrastructure
The common European data spaces (health, mobility, energy, industry, agriculture, finance, public administration, procurement) rely on the Data Spaces Support Centre, on Simpl, the Commission's open middleware, and on industry specifications: the IDSA Dataspace Protocol, the Eclipse Dataspace components and the Gaia-X trust framework [R54][R30].
### EHDS
The European Health Data Space (Regulation (EU) 2025/327) regulates the primary and secondary use of health data; the Commission must adopt its main implementing acts before 26 March 2027 [R55]. Health data access bodies and secure processing environments are likely to be public-order activities under the CADA, requiring level 2 or higher.
### Federation versus centralisation
Data spaces are federated: data remains at source and is shared under machine-readable usage policies, with connectors and verifiable credentials. This fits with the CADA because it reduces the need to concentrate data on large platforms and allows different assurance levels to be applied to each node.
### A data space node is infrastructure
Each data space participant needs a connector deployed on its own or trusted infrastructure, with identity, storage, security and monitoring. For the channel, this means demand for edge servers, secure storage and operation services, especially in industry, health, logistics and energy, where integrators already have customers.
### What it means for the value chain
Infrastructure vendors can work with their distributor to prepare data space node configurations (server, storage, security and open software) ready for integrators to deploy. Integrators with sector-specific solutions are sitting on data that is valuable for industrial, health or circular economy data spaces and can offer their customers participation with sovereignty guarantees. The team that authored this report has taken part in three federated data space demonstrators funded by Spain's Secretariat of State for Digitalisation (health, public procurement and circular economy), experience that can be transferred to the channel's customers.
> Data spaces are the "data" version of the sovereignty the CADA applies to the cloud: federation, control at source and verifiable trust, deployed by the channel.

## 15. Product regulation that already affects the distributor: WEEE, ecodesign and e-invoicing
### The CADA arrives on an already regulated channel
The technology distributor is no stranger to compliance. Every SKU it distributes must comply with rules on waste, efficiency, product safety and, increasingly, cybersecurity. Understanding these obligations is useful for two reasons: they show that the channel knows how to manage compliance at scale, and they provide processes that can be reused for sovereignty evidence.
### WEEE
In Spain, Royal Decree 110/2015 regulates waste electrical and electronic equipment (RAEE). Producers, including those who import and place equipment on the market for the first time, must register, declare the equipment placed on the market and finance waste management, usually through collective extended producer responsibility schemes; distributors must take back used equipment in certain cases [R56]. The producer's registration number must appear on invoices between producers and distributors. It is an example of how regulation already allocates obligations by link in the chain.
### Ecodesign of servers and storage
Regulation (EU) 2019/424 sets ecodesign requirements for servers and online data storage products, focused on power supply efficiency, active-state performance, material information, and ease of dismantling and secure data deletion for reuse; the regulation is under review [R57]. The review, together with the efficiency targets in Annex I of the CADA and the European data centre energy efficiency package [R58], points to more demanding requirements for the hardware sold to data centres.
### Refurbished equipment
The circular economy is growing in the channel: according to CONTEXT, sales of refurbished PCs through the distribution channel grew by 7 % in the fourth quarter of 2025 in the five largest European markets, and distributors, Esprinet included, have gained weight in this category [R59]. The CADA, which requires secure deletion and asset control as evidence, and ecodesign, which facilitates reuse, push in the same direction.
### E-invoicing
Spain approved Royal Decree 238/2026 on mandatory B2B e-invoicing and Order HAC/1028/2026 regulating the public solution; the VERI*FACTU system has been postponed to October 2028 to align it with the e-invoicing timeline for SMEs and the self-employed [R60]. It affects all the business management software sold by the channel and the invoicing processes of distributors and integrators.
### What it means for the value chain
The distributor already maintains, for thousands of SKUs, data on producer registration, efficiency, marking and warranties. Adding a sovereignty sheet (telemetry location, origin of support, components, control) is a natural extension of that system. For vendors, offering WEEE, ecodesign, CRA and CADA compliance in a single package will reduce sales friction. For integrators, equipment retirement, certified erasure and reuse services (ITAD) become part of the customer's sovereign life cycle.
> The channel already knows how to manage product compliance at scale. The CADA adds one more column to each SKU's datasheet: sovereignty.

## 16. The Spanish and Portuguese framework
### AI governance in Spain
Spain was a pioneer in Europe in creating a national AI supervisory authority, AESIA. On 26 May 2026 the Council of Ministers sent to Parliament the draft Organic Law on the proper use and governance of AI (Proyecto de Ley Orgánica para el buen uso y la gobernanza de la IA), which designated AESIA as the main authority, regulated sandboxes and penalties and included a title on the use of AI in the central government public sector [R61]. The bill lapsed with the dissolution of Parliament on 6 October 2026 (Royal Decree 806/2026), ahead of the general election of 29 November [R42]. Today there is no Spanish AI law: the AI Act applies directly and AESIA continues to operate, but the national penalty regime is missing. The next Government will have to present a new text.
### National Security Framework
Royal Decree 311/2022 regulates Spain's National Security Framework (Esquema Nacional de Seguridad, ENS), mandatory for the public sector and its technology suppliers. Its category system and certification of conformity are today the key to accessing the Spanish public cloud market and the natural basis for future CADA recognition.
### Digital sovereignty and data centres
On 24 February 2026 the Government presented the Roadmap to accelerate Digital Sovereignty, which places cloud and data centres at its core [R62]. On 27 August 2026 it put out to consultation a draft royal decree on sustainable data centres which, for facilities of 1 MW or more, required at least 80 % of each hour's consumption to be backed by renewables from that same hour and the operator to be established in the EU [R63]. After more than 600 submissions and a critical report from the CNMC on 22 September [R64], the revised text raises the threshold to 10 MW, allows a ramp-up to 80 % hourly renewables until 2031 and focuses sovereignty on data centres hosting public systems in the ENS HIGH category, whose data will have to remain in the EU. The Council of State endorsed it in November with four changes and the Government plans to approve it on 17 or 24 November 2026; as a royal decree, it did not lapse with the dissolution of Parliament [R65]. Part of the industry still opposes it; the Government presents it as a way of managing growth.
### Public programmes and the channel
The experience of the Kit Digital programme, with its network of digitalisation agents, showed that the channel is the most effective vehicle for bringing digitalisation to SMEs and the self-employed: Red.es delivered it through thousands of approved technology providers [R66]. It is reasonable to expect the national cloud and AI strategy required by the CADA to rely on similar mechanisms (our own analysis).
### Portugal
Portugal has a dynamic ICT sector, with more than 13,000 technology companies and around 245,000 professionals, and is consolidating its position as a nearshore services hub for European companies [R67]. For distribution, it is the market with the highest relative growth: the Esprinet Group grew there by 47 % in 2025 and by 37 % in the first half of 2026 [R17][R7]. Portugal will have to designate its CADA authority, adopt its cloud and AI strategy and complete its own transposition of NIS2.
### What it means for the value chain
Spain and Portugal could be among the first markets to apply the CADA swiftly: Spain has an AI authority, a security framework and a sovereignty roadmap. For vendors, ENS certification of their products and services will be a differentiating asset. For integrators, maintaining and raising their ENS certifications and following the future national strategy is a priority. For the value-added distributor, operating with a single organisation in both countries allows it to offer configurations validated for both national frameworks.
> The peninsula could be an early CADA market: it has a security framework, an AI authority, a sovereignty roadmap and a channel that has already proven its ability to deliver public programmes.

## 17. The European cloud market: dependency, concentration and sovereign demand
### A measurable dependency
European cloud providers went from 29 % of the European market in 2017 to 15 % in 2022, a share that has held steady, while three non-European hyperscalers hold more than 70 % [R1]. Synergy Research data for the first quarter of 2026 put AWS and Azure alone at around 65–70 % of EU cloud revenue [R47].
### Demand that is changing in nature
Gartner forecasts that European spending on sovereign infrastructure as a service will grow from USD 6.9 billion in 2025 to USD 12.6 billion in 2026 and approach USD 23.1 billion in 2027 [R68]. The study commissioned by the Commission for the impact assessment also quantifies the gap between existing computing capacity and projected demand [R69]. This is a structural change in the way of buying. The hyperscalers have responded with "sovereign" offerings and large investments in European data centres, such as the EUR 33.7 billion announced by AWS in Spain [R63].
### Computing capacity for AI
Bruegel stresses that the EU lags far behind the United States in AI chip capacity and warns that strict sovereignty criteria could make capacity more expensive [R70]. Data centres consumed around 68 TWh in the EU in 2024 and their demand could almost double by 2030 [R58].
### What the CADA is trying to change
The CADA does not seek to drive out the hyperscalers, but to create a market segment, the public one and by extension the critical one, where competition is also based on control and resilience. If that segment reaches critical mass, European providers will be able to scale and non-European ones will have incentives to offer European control and operating structures, as some alliances already do (S3NS between Thales and Google, or Bleu between Orange, Capgemini and Microsoft).
### Cloud is not only public
For the channel, one nuance is essential: sovereignty is also built outside the public cloud. Private clouds in the customer's data centre, clouds from regional providers, colocation and infrastructure-as-a-service consumption models (HPE GreenLake, Dell APEX or Lenovo TruScale) make it possible to offer control and localisation with hardware sold through the channel. Much of the sovereign demand will translate into purchases of servers, storage, networking, and virtualisation, data protection and security software, not into hyperscaler subscriptions.
### Where the value lies for the channel
The CADA tends to fragment infrastructure (more providers, more levels, more regions), and fragmentation increases the value of those who know how to orchestrate it. For infrastructure vendors, the sovereign segment is an opportunity to win back workloads that had moved to the public cloud. For integrators, the value lies in the services layer: migration, integration, hybrid operation, security and compliance. For the value-added distributor, the opportunity is to aggregate the pieces into reference offers per level.
> Market concentration will not disappear with the CADA, but a segment with its own rules will open up where size matters less than demonstrable control, and where the channel's hardware and services regain prominence.

## 18. The IT distribution market in Europe, Spain and Portugal
### Spain: a record year for distributors
According to CONTEXT data reported by Newsbook, distributor business in Spain grew by 15.1 % in 2025 [R6]. The momentum came from equipment refreshes ahead of the end of Windows 10 support in October 2025, from growth in data centre infrastructure and from cybersecurity. The Alimarket 2026 Report lists among the main distributors TD SYNNEX, Esprinet, Ingram Micro, MCR, Riba Mundo Tecnología, Inforpor, Depau, DMI, Infortisa and Globomatik, among others [R15].
### Europe: a more moderate 2026
For Europe as a whole, CONTEXT forecasts IT distribution growth of around 2.1 % in 2026 [R71]. The end of the Windows 10 effect and the memory shortage, which has driven early purchases and is putting pressure on prices in the second half, point to a more uneven year for the workplace segment [R72]. Data centre infrastructure, security and software keep growing.
### The channel is losing share but gaining influence
Omdia estimates that the share of IT spending channelled by partners will fall from 70.1 % in 2025 to 66.7 % in 2026, due to the growth of direct server sales to hyperscalers and of cloud software [R10]. At the same time, absolute spending through partners keeps growing, 82 % of IT spending by SMEs in EMEA goes through them [R11] and cybersecurity depends on the channel for more than 90 % [R12]. At Canalys Forum EMEA 2026 it was warned that up to 30 % of current partners will not exist in their current form by 2030, with growth concentrated in AI, cybersecurity and data specialists [R73].
### The distributor as orchestrator
The GTDC, the association of the large technology distributors of which Esprinet is a member [R74], argues in its July 2026 report that distribution can raise vendors' execution capacity above 80 % in key go-to-market functions, and that vendors prioritise visibility of return (88 %), strategic alignment (85 %) and partner recruitment and enablement (83 %) [R75]. In Spain, the Newsbook round table on cloud brought together Arrow, Ingram Micro, TD SYNNEX and V-Valley to analyse why the distributor remains essential in the cloud environment too [R76]. The GTDC report of March 2026 stresses that the distributor's value already goes well beyond logistics and credit: multi-vendor integration, subscription and consumption models, and partner enablement [R9].
### What it means for the value chain
The Iberian channel is growing faster than Europe, and the value segment is growing faster than the whole. This provides room to invest in preparing for the CADA. But Omdia's warning is clear: the channel that does not evolve towards services and specialisation will lose relevance. The CADA is an opportunity to accelerate that evolution with a regulatory reason that customers understand.
> The Iberian channel comes to the CADA on a growth path, but with an underlying message: share is won through specialisation and service, not volume. Sovereignty is a specialisation.

## 19. Vendor programmes in transformation
### 2026, the year partner programmes were redesigned
Almost all the large vendors redesigned their channel programmes in 2025 and 2026. The changes share three features: fewer tiers and more competencies, incentives linked to value and the customer life cycle, and greater weight for managed services and AI.
### The main changes
- From 1 November 2026 HPE is unifying the HPE and Juniper Networks programmes into HPE Partner Ready Vantage, with a single set of tiers, competencies and compensation, and is expanding the range of products it sells exclusively through the channel [R77]. In Spain, close to 90 % of the opportunities won by HPE Aruba Networking are identified by a partner [R78].
- In February 2026 Cisco launched the Cisco 360 Partner Program: it eliminates the Gold, Premier and Select tiers, creates two categories (Partner and Preferred Partner), measures the value delivered across the customer life cycle and adds specialisations in secure AI infrastructure and secure networking [R79].
- In the first quarter of 2026 Google Cloud launched its Partner Network with Select, Premier and Diamond tiers, competencies based on validated capability and contribution, and a six-month transition [R80].
- Microsoft tightened the requirements of its CSP programme and has brought channel sales to its marketplace [R49][R48].
- Dell presented its 2026 partner programme geared to new opportunities in AI and data centre [R81]; Lenovo describes itself as a channel company and has integrated its PC, server and services offering under a single approach; HP is driving equipment refresh programmes.
- Broadcom closed the VMware cloud service provider programme to many small European partners [R51].
### What they have to do with the CADA
None of these programmes yet includes sovereignty criteria in the CADA sense. But all of them are moving towards measuring the value that the partner delivers to the customer: competencies, services, life cycle. It is reasonable to anticipate (our own analysis) that, once the CADA applies, vendors will add "sovereign deployment" or "European public sector" competencies to their programmes, just as they now add security or AI competencies.
### What it means for the value chain
For vendors, the recommendation is to get ahead: define now which configurations in their catalogue can support levels 1, 2 and 3, and what evidence they will provide to the partner. For integrators, the growing complexity of the programmes makes a distributor that translates requirements, manages certifications and helps meet thresholds more valuable. For the value-added distributor, the opportunity is to become the point where vendors "upload" their sovereignty and integrators "download" it ready to use, with training and certification in its partner academies.
> Partner programmes already measure competencies and value. Sovereignty will be the next competency, and the distributor that standardises it first will set the format for the market.
